## Shared Kashi Khand background

The hub relates that King Divodasa ruled Kashi under an agreement with Brahma that celestial beings should stay away while the king governed well and protected pilgrims' religious pursuits. Shiva wanted to return to Kashi and first sent the Yoginis to create disruption, but they stayed because of the city's beauty. Surya was then sent to find fault with Divodasa's rule. He tried several disguises and found no fault, while becoming attracted to Kashi himself. He decided to remain and pursue religious activity. The hub cites Kashi Khand Chapter 9 for worship on Sankranti and auspicious Sunday/eclipses.

### Yama Aditya

**Geographic zone:** East Kashi / Sankata Ghat planning zone

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. Yama installed both a Shiva Linga, Yameshwar, and a Surya image, Yama Aditya, at Yama Tirtha and performed tapas there. The text says bathing at Yama Tirtha (identified by the site with the Ganga at Sankata Ghat) and worshipping both shrines frees a devotee from the torments of Yamaloka. It also describes an auspicious ancestral rite when Tuesday, Bharani nakshatra and Chaturdashi coincide. The verse places Yama Aditya west of Yameshwar and east of (Atma) Veereshwar.

**Address and navigation:** Address: Ck.7/135 on the steps leading to Sankata Ghat. Go by rickshaw to Chowk, then walk; Sankata Devi Temple is a landmark. Alternatively, arrive by boat at Sankata Ghat and climb the steps.

### Keshava Aditya

**Geographic zone:** North Kashi planning zone around Adi Keshav/Padodak; confirm precise shrine

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. Surya saw Adi Keshav (Vishnu) worshipping a Shiva Linga and asked why Vishnu, whom the world regards as the indwelling Lord, would worship Shiva. Vishnu explained that Shiva is supreme within Kashi and that Linga worship there is especially important; the account says that performing abhisheka and taking the water as prasada carries the merit of bathing at sacred tirthas. Surya accepted Adi Keshav as his teacher, and his form became Keshava Aditya. The text associates worship with supreme knowledge and liberation, and mentions bathing at Padodak Tirtha in Magha on a Sunday coinciding with Saptami as especially auspicious.

**Address and navigation:** The article has a location section; exact address and directions were not captured in this extraction. Check the shrine's relation to Adi Keshav and Padodak Tirtha before routing.

**Original source:** https://varanasitemples.in/category/aditya-temples/keshava-aditya/

### Mayukh Aditya

**Geographic zone:** North Kashi / Panchganga Ghat planning zone

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 49. Surya installed the Gabhasteeshwar Shiva Linga and Mangala Gauri Devi at Panchanada Tirtha (Panchganga Ghat), then undertook intense worship of Shiva. The heat of his rays grew so intense that ordinary life became difficult. Shiva and Parvati appeared; Shiva touched Surya and cooled him. Surya praised them and received boons. Because only Surya's rays were visible during the worship, Shiva named this form Mayukh Aditya, meaning effulgence. The article associates Sunday worship with protection from illness.

**Address and navigation:** The story places the shrine at Panchanada/Panchganga Ghat, but exact room/lane directions were not verified in the extracted section. Ask locally for Mayukh Aditya and confirm the specific shrine.

**Original source:** https://varanasitemples.in/category/aditya-temples/mayukh-aditya/

### Lolark Aditya

**Geographic zone:** South Kashi

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 46. Lolark Aditya is associated with Lolark Kund near the former Assi-Ganga confluence. The source describes special merit from bathing at the kund in Magha when Shukla Shashthi or Saptami falls on Sunday; it says the rite fulfils wishes and cleanses sins accumulated over seven births. Regular Sunday worship is also associated in the text with spiritual cleansing and relief from skin ailments. These are traditional religious claims, not medical advice.

**Address and navigation:** Near Lolark Kund and Tulsi Ghat. By road, go by rickshaw to Marwadi Seva Sangh in Assi and continue on foot. Alternatively, take a boat to Tulsi Ghat and climb the steps. The source says the pond is now a kund, although it was traditionally associated with the Assi-Ganga confluence.

**Original source:** https://varanasitemples.in/category/aditya-temples/lolark-aditya/

### Arun Aditya

**Geographic zone:** North/West/East/South zone: not safely established from the extracted location text

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. Vinata and Kadru were wives of Kashyapa. Vinata's first offspring, an owl, was rejected by other birds as an unsuitable king. Impatient for her remaining eggs to hatch, Vinata broke one open too early and found Arun formed from the thighs upward but without fully developed legs. Arun became angry at his mother's impatience and cursed her to become enslaved to Kadru's children, then relented enough to say the remaining egg would produce the son who could free her. Arun later came to Kashi seeking relief from his deformity and prayed to Surya. Surya appeared, blessed him and made him his charioteer. Surya promised that worship in this form would keep sadness and poverty away; the article also associates worship with freedom from illness, obstacles and sorrow.

### Draupad Aditya

**Geographic zone:** Zone not verified

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 49. During the Pandavas' exile, Draupadi was distressed by her husbands' hardship and came to Kashi to pray to Surya. Moved by her devotion, Surya gave her an inexhaustible vessel from which she could feed any number of guests, with the condition that she eat last. Surya also explained that Shiva had granted him the power to remove the sorrow of anyone who worshipped him here before going to Vishweshwar. The story links this shrine with relief from sorrow and freedom from hunger.

### Ganga Aditya

**Geographic zone:** South/riverfront planning zone; confirm locally

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. Surya is said to have taken up residence at a place in Kashi to worship the sacred Ganga. His form at the riverfront became known as Ganga Aditya; the account says he continues to praise the river and bless devotees. The text associates worship with protection from misfortune, deception and illness.

**Address and navigation:** Address given by source: No. 1/68, below Nepali Mandir, Lalita Ghat. The image is in a room behind an iron grill facing the Ganga. From Vishwanath Temple, walk toward the ghat steps and ask for Nepali Pasupatinath Temple; the shrine is below it. ### Khakholkh Aditya

Vinata lost the wager and became enslaved. Garuda sought to free his mother; the snakes demanded amrita, leading Garuda to undertake a dangerous quest and encounter Vishnu. ### Saamba Aditya

**Geographic zone:** Zone not verified

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand narrative begins in Dwaraka. Sage Narada visited and was warmly received by Krishna's sons, but Saamba failed to show him respect. Narada complained to Krishna and insinuated that Saamba's beauty might lead to improper behaviour. Krishna watched Saamba but found no misconduct. On a later visit, Narada created a situation in which Saamba entered Krishna's private quarters to announce Narada's arrival, inadvertently disturbing Krishna in the company of women. ### Uttarark Aditya

**Geographic zone:** Zone not verified; source discusses Uttarark Kund

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 47. The Brahmin Priyavrat and his wife Shubhavradha had a daughter, Sulakshana, whose birth under Moola star's first pada worried her parents about her marriage prospects. Her father's anxiety led to illness and death; her grieving mother also died. Sulakshana performed her parents' rites and began severe penance to Uttarark Aditya. A white goat repeatedly bathed at Uttarark Kund, waited nearby and drank from the kund. Shiva and Parvati later saw the emaciated girl praying and offered her a boon. ### Vimal Aditya

**Geographic zone:** West/Central Kashi planning zone; verify local zone

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. Vimal, who lived in a hilly region, was afflicted with leprosy, which the story attributes to earlier-life sins. He left home and came to Kashi, where he performed Surya worship, offerings of flowers and prescribed rites. Surya appeared, healed him and granted a boon requested by Vimal: that devotees should be spared the same disease and hardship. Surya promised to remain in the Vimal Aditya image and bless worshippers. The source states this as a scriptural belief; it is not medical guidance.

**Address and navigation:** Address: D-35/273, lane behind Khari Kuan, Jangambari, near Godowlia. Source says visitors can travel by rickshaw to the area and then walk into the lane.

**Original source:** https://varanasitemples.in/category/aditya-temples/vimal-aditya/

### Vriddh Aditya

**Geographic zone:** Central Kashi planning zone near Vishalakshi; exact lane to verify

**Puranic/Kashi Khand account (detailed paraphrase):** Kashi Khand Ch. 51. The elderly devotee Harit performed intense Surya worship near Vishalakshi Devi. When Surya appeared and offered a boon, Harit explained that tapas requires strength and youth, and asked to become young enough to pursue ascetic practice. Surya granted the request. The deity became known as Vriddh Aditya because an old devotee had praised him at that place. The source associates regular worship, especially on Sundays, with energy, accomplishment and freedom from misery.

**Address and navigation:** The source places Harit near Vishalakshi Devi. Exact street address and route were not captured in the extraction; use Vishalakshi as a broad landmark only, then confirm locally.

**Original source:** https://varanasitemples.in/category/aditya-temples/vriddh-aditya/
