THE PURANIC ADVENT OF LORD GANESHA IN KASHI

Kashi Khand • Chapter 17, Shloka 19 अङ्गारक चतुर्थ्यां तु पुरा जज्ञे गणेश्वर: । अत एव तु तत्पर्व प्रोक्तं पुण्य समृद्धये ॥
"In ancient times, Lord Ganesha manifested on this holy Angarak Chaturthi (Krishna Paksha Chaturthi falling on a Tuesday). Therefore, this sacred festival is declared supreme for multiplying virtuous prosperity and absolving all spiritual obstacles."

The Legend of King Divodas and the Astrologer Disguise

According to chapters 17, 56, and 57 of the Kashi Khand, while Lord Shiva resided in Mandarachal, Kashi was governed by a deeply pious, faultless King named Divodas. Under King Divodas's dharmic rule, every citizen lived in harmony, and by pact with Lord Brahma, celestial devas were restrained from interfering with the city.

Yearning to return to his beloved Avimukta Kshetra, Lord Shiva sent the sixty-four Yoginis, Lord Surya (the Dwadasha Adityas), and his Shiva Ganas to find some flaw in Divodas's administration. However, so captivated were they by the unparalleled beauty and serenity of Kashi that they all permanently settled in the city.

Finally, Lord Shiva summoned his brilliant son, Lord Ganesha (Vinayak). Assuming the guise of an elderly, revered astrologer, Lord Vinayak entered Kashi. He interpreted dreams and planetary alignments for the citizens, winning the complete trust of the Queen and subsequently King Divodas himself. Lord Vinayak advised the King that on the eighteenth day a holy Brahmin (Lord Vishnu in disguise) would arrive to impart profound spiritual knowledge. Heeding this advice, King Divodas installed a Shivalinga, abdicated his throne, and departed for the heavens, paving the way for Lord Shiva's triumphant return to Kashi.

Overjoyed, Lord Shiva proclaimed that Lord Ganesha would be worshipped as Dhundhi Vinayak at the gateway of Vishweshwar, and in eight concentric defensive rings forming the Fifty-Six Vinayakas (Chhappan Vinayak) to guard Kashi from every cardinal direction.

Lord Ganesha here stands as the divine notary and scribe of the cosmos who witnesses, confirms, and officially records that a pilgrim has successfully arrived in Kashi, taken bath in the sacred waters of Mother Ganga, and worshipped Lord Vishweshwar.

The sanctum is prominently referenced across multiple chapters of the Kashi Khand and the website's pilgrimage guides as a primary geographic anchor in Vishwanath Gali. Immediately behind Sakshi Vinayak stands Kalipriya Vinayak (D-10/50) and the sacred Manaprakameshwar and Koti Lingeshwar shrines.

Pilgrims traditionally purchase holy red sacred threads, sweets, and coconut offerings here, and offer prayers so that their meritorious deeds in Avimukta Kshetra remain eternally witnessed by Lord Ganesha.

2. Sacred Offerings and Ritual Guidelines

  • Auspicious Days: Tuesday (Mangalwar) and Wednesday (Budhwar) are the most sacred days for Vinayaka worship. Any Tuesday coinciding with Krishna Chaturthi (Angarak Chaturthi) is considered infinitely meritorious.
  • Traditional Offerings: Fresh Durva grass (21 blades tied with red thread), fresh Red Hibiscus flowers, Modak or Besan Laddoos, Sindoor (vermilion paste), and Ganga Jal.
  • Dhundhi Vinayak Protocol: By scriptural injunction in Kashi Khand Chapter 57, pilgrims must always bow before Dhundhi Vinayak before entering the Vishwanath Garbha Griha.
  • Sakshi Vinayak Custom: Pilgrims purchase red mauli (sacred protective thread) and declare: "O Sakshi Vinayaka, be Thou the eternal witness that I have arrived in holy Avimukta and bathed in the celestial Ganga!"
